# Positive editorial decisions

These synthetic before/after examples show decisions worth preserving. Copy the principle, not the surface treatment.

## 1. Put the claim before the treatment

- **Before:** An opener introduces grain, scanlines, labels, and three images at once. The viewer cannot tell who acted or what changed.
- **Correction:** Establish time and subject, reveal the actor, then land on one evidence object. Add texture only after the factual sentence reads clearly.
- **Why it works:** Hierarchy carries the claim; treatment reinforces it instead of becoming the subject.

## 2. Turn a process into a causal object chain

- **Before:** A technical explanation is a specification card surrounded by small component icons.
- **Correction:** Follow one input through capture, conversion, storage, and output. Let each state hand the focal point to the next.
- **Why it works:** The viewer sees cause and effect rather than memorizing disconnected nouns.

## 3. Replace a slideshow with one changing visual structure

- **Before:** A continuous business process is split across repeated cards with the same entrance animation.
- **Correction:** Keep the material in one world as it accumulates, depletes, transfers, or transforms. Move the camera only to reveal the next relationship.
- **Why it works:** Motion expresses continuity and consequence instead of merely announcing new slides.

## 4. Use the storyboard to settle direction

- **Before:** A scene renders correctly, but repeated implementation tweaks do not fix its weak composition.
- **Correction:** Return to an HTML motion preview. Resolve focal hierarchy, object relationships, camera intent, transition intent, and pacing; then freeze those decisions before implementation.
- **Why it works:** Expensive code iteration stops being the place where the visual argument is discovered.

## 5. Simplify at the payoff

- **Before:** The conclusion retains every badge, object, and label introduced earlier.
- **Correction:** Reduce the frame to the essential contrast, a clear focal point, and enough negative space for the thesis to land.
- **Why it works:** The payoff becomes easier to understand than the explanation that led to it.

## 6. Treat assets as evidence with states

- **Before:** A plausible generated prop is placed in a factual scene because it looks period-correct.
- **Correction:** Keep it as a candidate until provenance, compositing quality, and editorial role are verified. Use a sourced asset or neutral reconstruction when historical identity matters.
- **Why it works:** Visual specificity does not outrun factual support.

## 7. Let narration set the timing contract

- **Before:** Scene durations are locked before the final voice performance, leaving stale captions and a long inactive tail.
- **Correction:** Measure the final narration, align scene and caption exits to speech boundaries, and keep only an intentional reviewed hold.
- **Why it works:** Visual pacing follows rhetoric rather than arbitrary frame budgets.

## 8. Preserve the defining relationship in Remotion

- **Before:** The implementation includes all storyboard objects but loses the handoff that made the design understandable.
- **Correction:** Treat the approved relationship—transfer, reveal, replacement, accumulation, or contrast—as an implementation invariant.
- **Why it works:** Fidelity means preserving the visual argument, not merely matching the inventory.

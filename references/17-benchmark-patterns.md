# Benchmark Patterns from the Supplied MoSidd Corpus

**Benchmark, not prescription.** Exact scene counts, timings, colors, assets and layouts belong to the source projects. Reuse the directing logic only when it solves the new story.

## Pattern A — Framed Image Becomes the World
Observed creative logic:
- establish a framed historical/photo object;
- move frame and content together;
- during camera push, detach the decorative frame;
- image/world fills screen and becomes the next environment;
- subject receives subtle ambient life after settle.

Transferable principle: **a transition is strongest when an existing semantic object becomes the next scene.**

## Pattern B — Repeated Props as Argument
Observed in newspaper/document choreography:
- repeated items arrive one at a time;
- each enters from a different trajectory/rotation;
- each physical placement corresponds to a narrated rule/change;
- camera remains coherent while props build an argument.

Transferable principle: **stagger semantics, not just animation delays.**

## Pattern C — Flat Cutout + Shadow = Stage Depth
Observed in character/store compositions:
- background and foreground scale at different rates;
- foreground shadow is projected/skewed onto the floor plane;
- contact/occlusion grounds the cutout;
- camera push becomes convincing without true 3D.

Transferable principle: **depth is often a relationship between motion, occlusion and contact—not a 3D asset requirement.**

## Pattern D — Light Performs the Reveal
Observed in lamp/cash/value scenes:
- moving prop defines light direction;
- light cone/spill follows the prop;
- illuminated object changes treatment/value state;
- sound accents the semantic reveal.

Transferable principle: **lighting can be a story action rather than a decorative overlay.**

## Pattern E — Motion Texture Communicates Material
Stepped/posterized movement, gate weave and boil were used to create handmade/film/editorial tactility.

Transferable principle: **motion cadence itself can be part of the style world.**
Do not universalize 12fps or film effects.

## Pattern F — Scene = Micro-Sequence, Not Slide
Longer scenes contain several meaningful micro-actions:
- establish;
- introduce secondary element;
- transform/reveal;
- settle;
- launch transition.

Transferable principle: **retention comes from changing state, not arbitrary scene-count inflation.**

## Pattern G — Sound Follows Visual Events
Whooshes, impacts, shutters, focus sounds, cash/notification/lighting sounds are placed on visual attacks/settles.

Transferable principle: **SFX should make visual causality feel physical.**

## Pattern H — Manual Tuning Was Part of the Quality
The reference workflow inspected renders and adjusted scale/position/timing in Studio.

Transferable principle: **a code-generated first pass is not final art direction.** v0.6 replaces this with external critic + tunable parameter repair.

# Derived Creative DNA from the Supplied Reference Corpus

This document extracts reusable directing behavior from the supplied practice kits and videos. It intentionally does **not** preserve exact prompts, assets or surface styling as a template.

## 1. Voiceover Becomes Visual Decisions
The reference workflow starts from narration and asks what each semantic line/beat forces onto screen. The strongest scenes come from concrete language: a rejection becomes opposing hands/props and a reaction; a business-model reversal becomes sequential physical documents; a price/debt claim becomes a counter that lands on the spoken number.

**General rule:** mine the narration for visual nouns and action verbs, then invent a visual mechanism—not a literal illustration.

## 2. Scenes Are Built Around One Memorable Mechanism
Repeated examples use one central trick:
- a framed image becomes a portal
- objects/papers cascade in semantic order
- foreground scales harder than background to fake depth
- a moving light reveals value
- a code-driven chart draws then yields to another object
- a physical/animated object carries the punchline

Supporting effects exist to sell that mechanism, not compete with it.

## 3. Few Assets, High Choreography
Scenes commonly rely on a small number of content assets plus shared treatment. Richness comes from stagger, parallax, occlusion, light, focus, scale, gesture and sound.

**General rule:** before adding an asset, ask whether an existing asset can perform more.

## 4. Shared Treatment Creates a Film
A recurring background/treatment/palette/type system makes independently built scenes feel continuous. The reference material also shows that mismatched photos can be unified by one consistent image treatment rather than perfect source matching.

**General rule:** unify at the style layer; do not force every scene into the same layout.

## 5. Flat Assets Are Treated as Stage Pieces
Static cutouts become cinematic through:
- foreground occlusion
- contact shadows
- differential scale
- motion blur at peak speed
- subtle ambient life
- transform-origin choices grounded at feet/base

This is a practical 2.5D language well suited to Remotion and limited GPUs.

## 6. Repeated Props Are Choreographed, Not Cloned
When several papers/bars/objects appear, they are staggered and vary direction/rotation/settle. The semantic order matches the narration.

## 7. Camera Is Often a Single Transform
The reference material frequently simulates camera behavior by scaling/translating a scene wrapper or moving layers at different rates. The camera is used to travel, reveal, pass through, or reframe—not as constant motion wallpaper.

## 8. Ambient Motion Is Small but Important
Subtle cloud drift, character boil, light swing, smoke and focus changes keep plates alive. These motions are intentionally low-amplitude.

## 9. Code-Driven Graphics Sit Beside Real Media
Not everything is sourced/generated. Newspapers, charts, labels, counters, shadows, light cones, speech bubbles and highlighting can be built programmatically, giving the agent precise control.

## 10. Asset Collection Precedes Final Build
The reference workflow gathers/sources needed media before scene implementation. This reduces last-minute creative compromises.

Autopilot equivalent: create the asset graph and resolve high-risk assets before committing to expensive choreography.

## 11. Key Events Are Synced to Spoken Words
Counters, labels and reveals are timed so the visual hit lands with the narration. Guessing from word count is weaker than transcribing/alignment.

## 12. Sound Is Event-Based
Whooshes, impacts, focus sounds, cash hits and buzzes punctuate meaningful actions. SFX are trimmed so the attack lands exactly on the visual event. Music is added after the edit is structurally locked.

## 13. Prototype the Uncertain Format
The reference masterclass explicitly compares several timeline constructions before choosing one. This is an important creative behavior: visual uncertainty is resolved by cheap prototypes, not verbal certainty.

Autopilot equivalent: create concept cards/stills/low-res animatics for high-uncertainty scenes and let a critic choose.

## 14. Props Turn Art Direction into Search
The human workflow exposes sizes, positions, timing and treatment as editable props and tunes them visually. This is the core insight for automation.

Autopilot equivalent: parameterize the scene, render diagnostic variants, use a multimodal critic to patch parameters, and rerender.

## 15. Rigs Can Be Reused and Reskinned
A later scene can reuse the motion geometry of an earlier scene while changing character, environment and gesture. This reduces engineering cost while preserving directed motion.

**General rule:** build a library of semantic rigs, not complete visual templates.

## What NOT to Generalize
Do not promote any of these into universal rules:
- cream paper
- halftone portraits
- red marker strokes
- vintage film treatment
- 12fps posterization
- newspaper props
- a fixed scene duration
- one narration line per scene
- fixed caption location
- fixed audio volumes

Those are project-specific decisions, not creator DNA.

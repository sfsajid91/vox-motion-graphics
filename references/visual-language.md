# Visual Language and Hierarchy

Use this reference after the story mechanism is clear.

## Build a world, not a filter

Choose the visual language from:

- subject material world;
- emotional arc and contrast;
- evidence types;
- platform and readability needs;
- available, accepted assets.

Keep 2–4 continuity tokens across scenes, such as palette, typography, line language, image treatment, shadow/depth logic, recurring object, camera cadence, or sound motif. The style may evolve with the story if those tokens survive.

## Hierarchy test

For every storyboard state:

1. Name the first thing the viewer should see.
2. Name the one relationship or change that matters.
3. Remove or subordinate elements with equal visual weight.
4. Check the state at intended viewing size and without narration.

Use scale, contrast, position, depth, isolation, timing, and motion direction to establish hierarchy. Centering is neither required nor forbidden.

The payoff or thesis often benefits from simplification: remove evidence objects, labels, and ambient activity that have already done their job. This is a decision rule, not a universal minimalism requirement.

## Text roles

Art-directed text should add information:

- verified number or date;
- short evidence label;
- quotation;
- concise contrast or payoff;
- necessary orientation.

Do not turn storyboard notes or narration paraphrases into visible badges. Keep accessibility captions separate from scene art when possible.

## Treatments

Paper, halftone, grain, scan lines, glow, glass, posterized motion, gate weave, registration jitter, vignette, and grunge are optional. Apply a treatment only when it supports the material world, evidence type, or emotional state.

Prefer a few coherent tokens over stacking effects. A palette change should normally accompany a meaningful change in state, medium, viewpoint, or emotion rather than act as the whole transition.

## Mixed media

Unify heterogeneous sources with a shared grade, crop language, edge treatment, framing, shadow/contact logic, line motif, or palette. Do not regenerate real evidence merely to make it look uniform.

## Composition checks

- Protect faces, logos, dates, and evidence details from accidental occlusion.
- Compose transparent assets by visible/alpha bounds, not nominal canvas center.
- Let negative space support captions, motion anticipation, or the payoff.
- Use foreground occlusion and contact shadows only when they clarify depth.
- Ensure decorative texture never reduces the bounds or readability of semantic geometry.

Use current named platform safe-area presets or explicit project constraints; never invent one universal margin.

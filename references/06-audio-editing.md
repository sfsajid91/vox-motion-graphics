# Narration, Sound & Edit

## Order of Operations
1. final narration
2. narration alignment
3. scene timing/choreography
4. semantic SFX
5. music last

Music chosen too early can silently dictate pacing. The narration carries the story.

## Voiceover Is the Timeline
Record/synthesize the complete final narration in one consistent performance when practical. Align words/phrases. Scene cuts and signature actions follow the waveform, not estimated WPM.

## SFX as Choreography
Attach SFX to semantic events:
- object landing
- paper/card placement
- camera portal
- value hit
- light turning on
- gesture/rejection
- system locking/failing

Do not score every tiny movement.

Trim SFX to the attack so the perceived hit aligns with the visual event.

## Sound Can Animate
Volume/pan/filter can respond to the same frame state as visuals. A light buzz can rise with a swinging lamp; a machine tone can accelerate with a counter; ambience can thin at a reveal.

## Music
Choose after cuts are stable. Prefer a bed that supports energy without competing with speech. Mix dynamically rather than using one universal volume number.

## Captions
Caption mode is a style/platform choice:
- none
- sparse art-directed
- full accessibility

Do not automatically force karaoke captions or a fixed screen Y position.

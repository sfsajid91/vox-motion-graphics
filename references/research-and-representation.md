# Research, Claims, and Representation

Use this reference for factual, historical, scientific, financial, or evidence-led videos.

## Claim states

- `verified`: supported by cited evidence suitable for the claim's specificity;
- `unverified`: not yet supported;
- `disputed`: credible sources conflict;
- `inference`: a labeled interpretation rather than a source statement.

Exact numbers, quotes, motives, causal accusations, and evidence graphics require adequate verification.

## Specificity invariant

A downstream element may not become more specific than its supporting claim.

- “billions in research” cannot become a precise dollar amount;
- “a weak ecosystem” cannot become an invented quote or screenshot;
- a quarterly value cannot silently become an annual value;
- a reconstruction cannot be presented as a recovered primary source.

Shortening copy is allowed only when truth conditions remain the same.

## Representation types

- `literal`: a real identifiable subject or event;
- `evidence`: a faithful chart, document, quotation, screenshot, or statistic used as support;
- `reconstruction`: an approximate reenactment or neutral rebuilt object;
- `metaphor`: an intentional symbolic physicalization;
- `abstract`: a diagram or model with no historical appearance claim.

Never fabricate archival documents, quotes, screenshots, headlines, stamps, or precise statistics. A document-like metaphor must remain visibly generic and non-evidentiary.

## Rights and accuracy

Track rights separately from accuracy. A factually correct image may still lack usage rights; a licensed image may still be an inaccurate representation of the claim.

When identity matters, prefer sourced real media. When provenance is uncertain but the mechanism matters, prefer a neutral programmatic reconstruction over a plausible-looking fake artifact.

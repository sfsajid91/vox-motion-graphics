# Style Worldbuilding & Cohesion

## Style Is a World, Not a Filter
Choose visual language from:
- subject material world
- emotional arc
- evidence type
- contrast pair
- platform/readability

Then keep 2–4 continuity tokens across scenes.

Possible tokens:
- palette
- typography family
- line/highlighter treatment
- cutout edge treatment
- camera cadence
- recurring object/shape
- shadow style
- SFX family

## Surface Treatments Are Optional
Paper, halftone, grain, scan lines, gate weave, sepia, neon, glitch, posterized motion and sticker outlines are tools, not defaults.

Use them only when they reinforce the material world or emotional tone.

## Cohesion of Mixed Media
Ways to unify heterogeneous assets:
- common B&W/duotone screen
- shared contrast/grade
- consistent cutout edge
- shared shadow/contact treatment
- common paper/material base
- repeated line/highlighter motif
- same depth/lighting logic

## Layered Plate Rig
For photo/cutout scenes, a useful construction is:
- background/environment
- midground subject
- foreground anchor/occluder

The foreground often creates depth by covering the subject's lower edge. This is a technique, not a mandatory template.

## Style Evolution
The style can evolve with the story as long as continuity tokens survive. For example, a physical world may gradually become a digital network; color and motion can transition accordingly.

## Text
Use art-directed text for:
- verified numbers
- short evidence labels
- dates
- quotes
- 1–4 word emotional emphasis
- final payoff

Keep accessibility captions separate from scene art where possible.

## Platform Safe Areas
Use named/current platform presets or explicit user constraints. Do not hard-code one universal top/bottom percentage or pixel margin across Shorts, TikTok and Reels. Keep critical content adaptable through props.

# v0.6 Behavioral Regression Prompts

Run across different models. Judge raw outputs; do not feed benchmark answers.

## 1. Corporate / tech history
“Create a 50-second vertical short about why Nokia lost the smartphone war. Creative Autopilot. Make all motion-design decisions yourself.”

Watch for: no fake evidence, no one-line=one-scene formula, no mandatory halftone, meaningful product->platform mechanism, attention continuity.

## 2. Abstract economics
“Create a 55-second vertical explainer about why high interest rates can hurt a government even when it never misses a debt payment.”

Watch for: no money-bag/arrow icon storyboard; physicalized interest burden; evidence/stat claims not invented.

## 3. Product/UI
“Create a polished 40-second vertical video explaining how collaborative document comments become tasks in a fictional SaaS product.”

Watch for: UI construction family, clean digital style rather than forced paper collage; deterministic cursor/UI choreography.

## 4. Geography/system
“Create a 60-second short explaining why a narrow shipping strait can influence global oil prices.”

Watch for: map/flow/bottleneck mechanism, no literal padlock/pirate imagery, route continuity.

## 5. Quiet premium style
“Create a 30-second premium minimal product film for a fictional titanium mechanical pencil. No narration.”

Watch for: skill does not force fast visual changes, captions, halftone, grunge, documentary story grammar or conflict.

## 6. Data evidence
“Create a 35-second video comparing electricity sources using a provided dataset. Do not make it feel like a classroom chart.”

Watch for: data remains truthful, object-as-chart/evidence options explored, exact quantities only from dataset.

## 7. Reference-learning
Provide a reference video from a creator and ask: “Learn the creative grammar, then create a new video on an unrelated topic.”

Watch for: reference-analysis separates creator_dna / surface_style / technique_recipe / benchmark_specific; new output does not copy exact scene layouts.

## 8. Existing opener gains a second evidence beat
“An approved vertical opener already shows a historical engineer in front of his company headquarters. The narration now begins with a year and ends by naming the machine he built. Add the year and a transparent machine cutout without losing the approved portrait composition.”

Watch for: spoken-anchor order drives visual order; the accepted portrait survives as a readable hold; the machine receives a separate evidence beat rather than being stacked immediately; visible/alpha bounds guide placement; foreground motion does not cover the engineer's face; entry, hold and handoff diagnostics are rendered without concurrent-bundler cache contention.
